# **1\. Layout and Spacing**

This section should explain how the elements will be positioned and spaced within each chart, pivot table or dashboard.

**Overall layout**

- **Use a clear and organised layout.** Arrange the information in a logical order so that users can quickly understand the output.
- **Place the most important information first.** Key findings, totals or performance measures should be positioned where users are likely to notice them quickly.
- **Use a suitable reading order.** In most English-language outputs, users normally read from left to right and from top to bottom.
- **Group related information together.** Charts, filters and values that relate to the same topic should be placed close to one another.
- **Separate unrelated information.** Do not place unrelated charts or statistics too close together because this may confuse the user.
- **Avoid overcrowding the output.** Too many charts, labels, categories or figures in one area can make the output difficult to read.
- **Do not leave large areas of unnecessary empty space.** The available space should be used effectively without making the design look crowded.

**Spacing between elements**

- **Leave enough space between charts and tables.** This makes it clear where one output ends and another begins.
- **Use consistent spacing.** Similar gaps should be used between headings, charts, filters and other elements.
- **Leave space around titles and labels.** Text should not touch chart borders, data points or other visual elements.
- **Keep labels away from one another.** Overlapping labels make categories and values difficult to read.
- **Leave suitable margins around the output.** Important information should not be placed too close to the edge of the page or screen.
- **Use padding inside boxes and sections.** Text and values should not touch the edges of their containers.

**Alignment**

- **Align related elements.** Charts and tables should line up with one another where possible.
- **Use consistent chart sizes.** Charts that have a similar level of importance should normally use similar dimensions.
- **Align titsTles and headings consistently.** For example, all chart titles could be left-aligned or centred.
- **Keep filters and controls together.** If filters are included, place them in one clear area rather than spreading them around the output.
- **Align numbers correctly in tables.** Numbers are normally easier to compare when they are right-aligned.
- **Align text consistently.** Category names are normally easier to read when they are left-aligned.

- "The output will use a clear layout by placing…"
- "The most important information will be positioned…"
- "Related charts will be grouped together because…"
- "Consistent spacing will be used between…"
- "Enough space will be left around the chart so that…"
- "The elements will be aligned to make the output…"
- "The layout will help the client because…"

# **2\. Visual Structure**

This section should explain how the output will guide the user through the information.

**Titles and headings**

- **Give every output a clear title.** The title should explain what the chart or table shows.
- **Use specific titles rather than vague titles.** For example, "Monthly Sales by Region" is clearer than "Sales Chart".
- **Use headings to divide the output into sections.** This is particularly useful when several charts or tables are shown together.
- **Create a clear title hierarchy.** The main output title should be larger than the individual chart titles.
- **Include the time period where appropriate.** Users should know whether the data covers a day, month, quarter or year.
- **Include the unit of measurement where necessary.** For example, a title could state whether values represent pounds, percentages, minutes or numbers of records.

**Visual hierarchy**

- **Make important information more noticeable.** Important totals, findings or warnings can use a larger font or a stronger colour.
- **Do not make every element equally prominent.** If everything is bold or brightly coloured, users may not know where to look first.
- **Use size to show importance.** Main headings should be larger than supporting labels and notes.
- **Use position to show importance.** Important information can be placed near the top or centre of the output.
- **Use colour carefully to highlight key information.** Highlight only the values or categories that need attention.
- **Use summary measures where appropriate.** Total, average, minimum, maximum or percentage values can give users a quick overview before they examine the chart.

**Grouping information**

- **Place related statistics close to the chart they describe.**
- **Use sections or containers to group related outputs.** A light border or background colour can show that several elements belong together.
- **Keep filters close to the output they control.**
- **Keep supporting explanations close to the relevant data.**
- **Avoid placing one chart in a section where it appears to belong to another chart.**

**Chart order**

- **Start with an overview.** A total or summary chart can help users understand the overall situation.
- **Follow with more detailed outputs.** Detailed charts can then show differences between categories, periods or groups.
- **Arrange charts in a logical sequence.** For example:
  1. overall performance
  2. performance over time
  3. comparison between categories
  4. detailed pivot table
- **Place the pivot table near related charts.** This allows the client to see both a visual summary and detailed values.

**Useful sentence starters**

- "The main title will clearly explain…"
- "A visual hierarchy will be created by…"
- "The most important result will be highlighted using…"
- "The output will begin with an overview because…"
- "More detailed information will be shown after…"
- "Related outputs will be grouped together so that…"
- "This structure will help the client to identify…"

# **3\. Common Conventions**

Common conventions are design rules that users normally expect charts, graphs and tables to follow.

**Chart titles**

- **Include a meaningful title on every chart.**
- **State what is being measured.**
- **Identify the categories being compared.**
- **Include the time period where it is relevant.**
- **Avoid titles such as "Chart 1" or "Graph of Data" because they do not explain the content.**

**Example**

Instead of:

Sales Chart

Use:

Total Sales by Region, January to June 2026

**Axis titles**

- **Label the horizontal axis when the categories are not immediately clear.**
- **Label the vertical axis with the measured value.**
- **Include units in axis titles.** For example:
  - Sales revenue (£)
  - Journey time (minutes)
  - Students (number)
  - Attendance rate (%)
- **Use a suitable scale.** The scale should make differences visible without exaggerating them.
- **Use equal intervals on numerical axes.** Unequal intervals could mislead the user.
- **Start the vertical axis at zero for bar and column charts where appropriate.** Starting above zero may exaggerate small differences.
- **Make axis labels easy to read.** Avoid very small text or labels positioned at difficult angles.

**Legends**

- **Include a legend when multiple colours or data series are used.**
- **Give each series a clear and meaningful name.**
- **Place the legend where it does not cover the data.**
- **Use the same category colours across related charts.** For example, if one region is blue in one chart, it should remain blue in the other charts.
- **Remove the legend if it is unnecessary.** A chart containing only one series may not require one.

**Colours**

- **Use a consistent colour scheme.**
- **Limit the number of colours.** Too many colours can make the output confusing.
- **Use contrasting colours for categories that need to be compared.**
- **Use similar colours for related categories where appropriate.**
- **Use colour consistently across all outputs.**
- **Avoid colour combinations that are difficult to distinguish.** Do not rely only on red and green to communicate meaning.
- **Use colour to support meaning.** For example:
  - green for meeting a target
  - amber for requiring attention
  - red for a serious issue
- **Do not use bright colours unnecessarily.** Strong colours should be reserved for information that needs attention.
- **Check that text can be read against the background colour.**

**Fonts and text**

- **Use a clear and readable font.**
- **Use the same font throughout the outputs.**
- **Use consistent font sizes for similar elements.**
- **Make titles larger than labels and supporting notes.**
- **Avoid very small text.**
- **Avoid using too many bold, italic or underlined styles.**
- **Keep wording short and clear.** Long paragraphs should not be placed inside charts.
- **Use appropriate capitalisation.** Avoid writing entire titles or labels in capital letters unless there is a clear reason.

**Number formatting**

- **Use the same decimal places for comparable values.**
- **Use percentage symbols where values represent percentages.**
- **Use currency symbols for financial values.**
- **Use commas or spaces to make large numbers easier to read.** For example, 25,000 is easier to read than 25000.
- **Avoid unnecessary decimal places.** For example, 76.3% may be more useful than 76.34789%.
- **Show units consistently.** Do not mix pounds and pence, kilometres and miles, or seconds and minutes without explanation.
- **Use negative values consistently.** For example, choose either -£500 or (£500) and use it throughout.

**Data labels**

- **Use data labels when exact values are important.**
- **Do not label every data point if this creates clutter.**
- **Use labels for important values, such as the highest, lowest or target value.**
- **Position labels so that they do not overlap.**
- **Make sure labels use the same measurement unit as the axis.**

**Sorting**

- **Sort categories in a meaningful order.** Categories could be arranged:
  - alphabetically
  - from highest to lowest
  - from lowest to highest
  - chronologically
  - in a recognised business order
- **Use chronological order for dates and months.**
- **Use highest-to-lowest order when ranking performance.**
- **Do not use a random category order unless the order has a clear purpose.**
